01 — The Research

Two diagnoses, two different problems

ADHD and autism get grouped together constantly in app marketing. The actual research says they break down almost opposite ways — which matters, because a mechanism that helps one can be neutral or even counterproductive for the other.

ADHD

The struggle is self-regulation, not the routine itself

  • Mornings and homework are specifically where it breaks down — not evening or meal routines.
  • Kids show more frustration completing routines than neurotypical peers doing the same tasks.
  • They receive more parental reprimands along the way — which becomes its own feedback loop.
  • Underlying issue is planning and time-management, not unwillingness.
  • Families report needing continuous supervision, more impatience, and being late for work more often.
Autism

The struggle is the transition, not the task

  • One 2026 study found routine execution itself comparable to neurotypical kids — comparable frustration, no gap in morning/meal/evening completion.
  • The much larger body of work on autism specifically points to moving between activities as the friction point, not doing any one activity.
  • Unpredictability is the trigger — abstract time concepts and sudden change, not the task's difficulty.
  • Visual timers that show time physically disappearing measurably help.
  • First/then sequencing and advance notice reduce transition time and challenging behavior.

02 — The Mapping

Where the app already answers this — and where it doesn't yet

Reading today's build (and everything before it) against those two hurdle profiles specifically, not against "ADHD/autism" as one blended thing.

ADHD · Reprimand loop

Continuous supervision and reprimands during routines create a negative feedback cycle that compounds frustration.

Already built

Instant celebration + points on every standard mission, plus a single end-of-window nudge to the parent instead of constant supervision — the whole design brief from the start was "don't let this become more nagging."

ADHD · Planning & time management

The deficit is executive function, not motivation — kids need external structure, not more willpower asked of them.

Already built

Time-ordered mission list with a "current task" highlight and per-task nudge toast — the app carries the planning load instead of asking the kid to hold it in their head.

ADHD/ASD · Kids who "don't do a lot" need to feel rewarded too

A reward curve that only pays off for high-effort kids teaches low-effort kids that trying doesn't matter.

Built today

The level curve was explicitly re-tuned so a couple of tasks ever already reaches Level 2–3 — the fast early payoff is deliberate, not a side effect.

Parent trust

A reward system a kid can trigger without doing anything erodes the exact trust the app exists to build.

Built today

Epic Feats now require a parent to confirm before the bonus points post — a lighter-touch trust mechanism than a reprimand, on top of the existing anti-cheat ledger that already stops checkbox-farming on standard missions.

Clinical hand-off

The family-climate-stress data point in the research is exactly what a parent needs to be able to hand to an OT or paediatrician — but raw stress isn't a deliverable.

Built today

Self-reported mood/focus/energy check-ins now feed a Progress Report with a real PDF export — closing the loop from "we're struggling" to "here's the data" that almost nothing else in this space does.

Autism · Transition predictability

The literature is specific here: it's not the schedule, it's the moment of switching — and a visual, physically-shrinking countdown is the single most evidence-backed tool for it.

Gap — see recommendations

The current nudge is a toast notification at the moment a task is due. There's no visual countdown building up to that moment, which is specifically what the transitions research calls out as the mechanism that works.

05 — Next Phase

What's worth doing before this ships

Now

A visual transition countdown

The single most evidence-backed gap on the board. A shrinking visual timer (and optional soft chime) in the few minutes before a task's scheduled time — not just the toast that fires at the moment it's due. This is a natural extension of the nudge system already built, not a new subsystem.

Grounded in Indiana Resource Center for Autism's transitions research — visual timers measurably reduce transition time and challenging behavior.
Next

Consider named routine groupings

Sense's entire pitch is bundling tasks into labeled routines ("Morning," "Bedtime") instead of a flat time-ordered list. The current mission list already reads as one implicit routine via time-ordering — this is a lighter lift than it sounds, and worth a look once the countdown work lands, not before.

Next

Lean into the clinician hand-off as the actual pitch

It's the clearest edge over everything except Goally. Getting even one OT or psychologist to look at the Progress Report format before launch — and eventually exploring anything like Goally's ESA-eligibility angle — turns a nice feature into the headline differentiator.

Deliberately not doing

Smart-home integration or real-money rewards

Both pull toward S'moresUp's or BusyKid's lane, which is a different product with a different value proposition. Chasing either dilutes the clinical/intrinsic-motivation positioning that's the actual reason this app is different from the field above.

One honest caveat: the 2026 Frontiers study found autistic kids' routine execution comparable to neurotypical peers in that specific sample — the transitions-are-the-real-hurdle read comes from the broader, older body of transition research, not that one study. Both are cited above rather than blended into one claim. As with the app's own onboarding tips and Progress Report, this is directional research, not a clinical guarantee.
