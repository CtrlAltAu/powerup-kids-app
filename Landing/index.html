Grounded in the research

Built around what actually helps kids with attention and transition differences.

Not a therapy replacement — a tool built around the same structural ideas clinicians and autism resource centers already recommend.

🔔

Visual, predictable transitions

Every mission shows up with a time, an icon, and a heads-up before it starts — the same "warn before you switch activities" approach autism resource centers recommend for easing the hardest part of a routine: the moment one thing ends and another begins.

— Indiana Resource Center for Autism, "Transition Time"
⭐

Reinforcement first

Every mission completed earns something — points, feats, play. No losses, no penalties, no red marks. Major health bodies recommend positive-reinforcement behavior strategies as the first-line approach for kids with ADHD, ahead of anything else.

— CDC, "Behavior Therapy First for Young Children with ADHD"
🎛️

Sensory-aware by default

Calmer color states, a "Calm Celebrations" toggle for kids who find confetti and sound overwhelming, and no flashing surprise animations — following published sensory-design guidance built specifically for neurodivergent users.

— Tiimo & Aspect Autism-Friendly Design Guide
Where we're being careful: the research on visual schedules and routine apps is real but mixed — strong for some outcomes (transitions, task initiation), weaker for others, and it varies a lot by kid. PowerUp Kids is a structure-and-motivation tool, not a diagnosis, treatment, or a substitute for an OT, therapist, or your pediatrician. Think of it as something that sits alongside that support, not instead of it.

Inside the app

Real screens. Real family using it. No mockups.

Every screenshot below is the actual app, mid-use — this is what a parent and kid see, not a rendering.

🎯 Missions

Chores, reframed as missions worth showing up for.

A parent builds the routine once — wake-up, homework, bath, whatever the day needs — and the kid works through it as missions with a level curve deep enough that it doesn't cap out after a good week.

Epic Feats sit above the daily list for the bigger wins — "broke a mental block," "zero-complaint day" — confirmed by a parent, not self-reported, so the points mean something.

  • A thousand-level curve, not a streak that resets and demoralizes
  • Parent-confirmed feats, so the reward system can't be gamed
  • Works for one kid free, multiple kids on Premium
📊 Progress Report

The one screen you can hand to someone.

Most habit apps stop at a streak count. This one tracks mood, focus, and energy alongside every mission, then surfaces the pattern for you — like flagging that "Free Time Unlocked" has quietly been the hardest mission of the week.

Export it as a PDF. Hand it to a teacher, a therapist, or an OT. It's built from your family's own data, and no one sees it unless you share it.

  • 7/14/30-day views with real per-mission completion rates
  • Plain-language insight tips, not just raw numbers
  • A private export — never posted, never shared automatically
🎮 Mini-games

Play time you earn, not chores you buy.

Finishing missions unlocks short, tuned mini-games — no ads, no in-game purchases nudging a kid to spend. The reward is play itself, which keeps the motivation intrinsic instead of turning routines into a shop.

Every game ships with a level system and a flap/tap budget instead of a punishing timer, so a kid who needs a slower pace still gets to finish the level.

  • Non-monetary rewards, on purpose
  • No ads, no third-party trackers, anywhere in the app
  • Calm, forgiving difficulty curves — not twitch-reflex punishing

Questions

Good to know before you start.

Is this a substitute for therapy or an OT?

No. PowerUp Kids is a structure-and-motivation tool built around the same principles clinicians already recommend — it's meant to sit alongside professional support, not replace it. If your family is working with a therapist or OT, the Progress Report is built to be something you can bring to that conversation.

What do we need to run it?

Any phone, tablet, or computer with a browser — it works today at powerup-kids-app.web.app. Dedicated iOS and Android apps are on the way.

Is my kid's data safe?

There are no ads and no third-party analytics anywhere in the app. Data is used to run the app itself — nothing else. A child's real name and profile are never made public, even on the worldwide leaderboard, which only ever shows an auto-generated nickname. Full details are in the privacy policy.

Can my kid see or change billing, or their sibling's stuff?

No. A kid's device can only ever update their own missions and play data — routines, settings, and billing stay parent-only, enforced at the database level, not just hidden in the UI.

Does it get harder to keep up as my kid grows?

The level curve runs deep on purpose — a great week doesn't cap it out — and Premium routines are fully editable any time a kid's needs change, so the app can grow with them instead of getting stale.
