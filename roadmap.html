Foundations & strategy Done

Researched the competitive landscape, Apple's Kids Category rules, COPPA/Australian privacy requirements, and realistic build costs. Locked in the four decisions above.

1
Branding & positioning Deferred

Decided to rename away from "Koda" and lean hard into gamification for the public product, but parked the exact name to keep momentum. Front-runner so far: PowerUp Kids (checked clear of direct conflicts).

→ Noted: a direct competitor already exists — QuestKids (questkids.io), "gamified behavior for happy families." Worth studying before launch.
2
Multi-family architecture In progress

One parent account (email + Sign in with Apple) can hold multiple child profiles, each with a fully editable routine and bonus feats instead of the fixed 8-task Koda schedule.

→ Free tier: one child, a basic plain checklist, no confetti/hype/custom feats, reminders only while the app is open. → Premium: full gamified experience, real push notifications, unlimited kids, custom feats/rewards, progress history.
3
Capacitor build wrapper Not started

Package the web app into native iOS and Android app shells, wired up with real haptics, notifications and sound through Capacitor plugins.

4
Parent setup & routine builder Not started

The screen where a parent builds their own child's tasks, times and bonus feats. This is what makes the app sellable beyond one family.

5
Free vs subscription paywall Not started

Gate the full feature set behind a subscription, wired into Apple and Google's in-app purchase systems.

6
Privacy & Kids Category compliance Not started

Write the privacy policy and check the app against Apple's Kids Category rules and children's privacy law: no third-party ads or analytics, no data sold on, parental gates on anything external.

7
Developer accounts Not started

Set up the Apple Developer Program and Google Play Console accounts, step by step, since you're starting from zero.

8
Store assets & build pipeline Not started

Icon, screenshots, store description, and a cloud build pipeline (Xcode Cloud or Codemagic) so neither of us needs to own a Mac.

9
Submit & launch Not started

Submit to App Review and the Play Store, respond to any review feedback, and go live.

Next up

Building the customizable core

The free/premium line is now the product's spine: free proves the app works, premium is where all the fun and the real reminders live. Next concrete steps:

  • Design the actual data structure for parent accounts + multiple child profiles + editable routines
  • Rebuild the app's UI so a parent can add/edit/reorder their child's tasks and feats, instead of the fixed Koda schedule
  • Wire up the free/premium gate around gamification, push notifications, and multi-kid profiles
